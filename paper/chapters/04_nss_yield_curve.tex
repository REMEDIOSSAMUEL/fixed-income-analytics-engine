\section{Six parameters and factor loadings}\label{sec:nss-equations}
Let \(T>0\) denote curve maturity in years and
\(\theta=(\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3,\tau_1,\tau_2)\) the parameter vector.
The four \(\beta\) coefficients are decimal rates; \(\tau_1,\tau_2>0\)
are decay scales in years, never payment-time variables.
A factor loading is a maturity-dependent coefficient multiplying a model
parameter. For dimensionless argument \(x>0\), define dimensionless loadings
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loadings}
L_1(x)=\frac{1-e^{-x}}{x},\qquad L_2(x)=L_1(x)-e^{-x}.
\end{equation}
With \(x_1=T/\tau_1\) and \(x_2=T/\tau_2\), the generic NSS decimal rate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nss}
r_{\mathrm{NSS}}(T;\theta)=\beta_0+\beta_1L_1(x_1)
+\beta_2L_2(x_1)+\beta_3L_2(x_2).
\end{equation}

\section{Asymptotic limits and interpretation}\label{sec:nss-limits}
An asymptotic limit describes the value approached as an argument tends to
a specified boundary. For fixed positive decay scales,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nss-limits}
\lim_{T\downarrow0}r_{\mathrm{NSS}}(T)=\beta_0+\beta_1,\qquad
\lim_{T\to\infty}r_{\mathrm{NSS}}(T)=\beta_0.
\end{equation}

\section{Calibration objective and residuals}\label{sec:calibration}
Calibration means selecting parameters to fit supplied observations.
Let \(n\) be the observation count, \(u_\ell>0\) observation maturity in years,
and \(z_\ell\) its decimal rate, for \(\ell=1,\ldots,n\).
The residual \(e_\ell\) is observed minus fitted rate.
Define the residual sum of squares \(S(\theta)\), feasible parameter set
\(\Theta\), fitted parameter vector \(\widehat\theta\), and
root mean squared error (RMSE) by
\begin{align}
e_\ell(\theta)&=z_\ell-r_{\mathrm{NSS}}(u_\ell;\theta),&
S(\theta)&=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}e_\ell(\theta)^2,\label{eq:residuals}\\
\Theta&=\mathbb R^4\times[0.01,100]^2,&
\text{objective: }&\min_{\theta\in\Theta}S(\theta),\label{eq:nss-objective}\\
\mathrm{RMSE}&=\sqrt{\frac{S(\widehat\theta)}n},&
\mathrm{RMSE}_{\bp}&=10^4\mathrm{RMSE}.\label{eq:rmse}
\end{align}
Here \(\mathbb R\) denotes the real numbers. The decay bounds in \(\Theta\) are in years. Least squares minimises squared
residuals; joint fitting is nonlinear because decay scales enter exponentials.
The returned \(\widehat\theta\) is a selected numerical candidate,
not an assertion that the global minimum has been found.
